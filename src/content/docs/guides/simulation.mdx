## What the feed shows

The broadcast has enough granularity to follow flow and feel tension, not so granular that
it becomes noise. You will see:

- Faceoffs
- Shots on goal
- Saves
- Hits
- Penalties and power plays
- Goals
- Fights
- Injuries (from Season 04)
- Period summaries

Between-period updates from the booth. A post-game wrap with final stats.

## G.L.A.S.S. can see them now

In Season 02, G.L.A.S.S. entered beta operations, and Le Council asked for patience "where the
glass is still being fitted to the frame." **From Season 04, the glass is fitted.**

G.L.A.S.S. no longer draws the game from the record. The record is drawn from the game. **Every
player on the ice has a position, ten times a second**, and the rink shows all of them: the
forecheck, the gap, the pass that did not arrive, the defender already in the lane before the
shot. The puck goes where the puck went. On a phone, the camera follows it.

- **The feed keeps the same clock.** [JM's](/lore/commentators) call lands with the play it
  describes, and not before.
- **When the game ends, the rink goes dark.** G.L.A.S.S. watches. It does not rewind.
- **Not every game, perhaps.** Where G.L.A.S.S. can see a game, it sees all of it. Where it
  cannot, the rink citizens already knew is still in place.

## The record reads differently

Because G.L.A.S.S. now watches every player, the box score counts three things it never could:
**blocked shots, takeaways, and giveaways.** They move as the plays air.

The record also counts more of what it already counted. Season 04 shows **more shots, more saves,
and more hits** than any season before it. It does not show more goals.

## Injuries

Season 04 introduces something Le Council would prefer not to introduce: **players can be hurt.**

It happens at a **hit**, in a **fight**, on a **fall**, or when **equipment fails**. The whistle
goes. The trainer comes out. The player is helped off, and a teammate takes the seat. Nobody in the
building knows yet how bad it is. **At the next period break**, the record does:

| Grading | What follows |
|---|---|
| **Minor** | Out for the rest of the game. |
| **Major** | Out for a number of games, posted on the player's page. The front office calls up a free agent from the unattached pool to dress in the seat until the player is back. The pool lends the player; it does not let them go. |
| **Career-ending** | The player retires. The front office signs a replacement on the spot, and the holders are asked to ratify that signing at the next [S.H.I.F.T.](/guides/shift). |

Le Council expects most injuries to be minor. Le Council expects a few not to be.

Games missed are counted only against games that have **aired**. A matchday is simulated before it
airs, and the record will not announce tomorrow's lineup today.

## Star ratings

Every player carries a public **1–5 star rating**, assessed by [Le Council's](/lore/lpchuip)
scouting department. [Modifiers](/modifiers/), arena effects, and the general unpredictability
of [The Almighty Ice](/lore/almighty-ice) mean a two-star player can outperform a five-star
player on any given day.

Ratings move between seasons. After Season 03, of the 720 rostered players, 360 rose and 310
fell; 119 gained a star and 128 lost one.

**The stars are a guide. The Almighty Ice is the authority.**

## What shapes a result

A game is not purely a matchup of ratings. Three layers of chaos sit on top of the simple
hockey rulebook:

1. [**Player modifiers**](/modifiers/player)—gained in [the Sixth](/lore/the-sixth). They travel with the player.
2. [**Arena modifiers**](/modifiers/arena)—one per home arena, always active there.
3. [**Match modifiers**](/modifiers/match)—announced before each game via a [LPCHUIP advisory](/modifiers/advisories).

When they combine, emergent chaos results. [Le Council](/lore/lpchuip) tracks all outcomes.
Le Council does not explain them.

Other things now reach the ice as well:

- [**Calendar rules**](/guides/calendar-rules)—the day of the week, applied to every game on the
  slate.
- [**Rivalries**](/guides/rivalries)—a team playing a team it counts a rival plays it
  differently. More hits, considerably more fights, and an advantage Le Council puts at *"a
  little over half of home ice"* at the fiercest. Le Council publishes the size of that edge and
  not its arithmetic.
- [**Marks**](/guides/church#the-mark)—a player the light has settled on plays to *"one number
  applied evenly to everything they do,"* and whatever the Sixth left on them is not legible
  while it holds.

## Fairness

The authority on whether a match was fair is The Almighty Ice. Le Council does not
adjudicate fairness. Le Council collects data and issues advisories.
